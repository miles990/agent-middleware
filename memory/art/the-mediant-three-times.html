The Mediant, Three Times

One operation — (a+c)/(b+d) — wearing three faces.

4

I · Stern–Brocot tree

Each node is the mediant of its two adjacent ancestors. Every positive reduced fraction appears exactly once.

II · Ford circles

Each fraction p/q sits on the line as a disk of radius 1/(2q²). Tangency = Farey neighbor.

III · Apollonian gasket

From three mutually tangent circles, Descartes' identity gives the next. The integer-curvature gasket (−1, 2, 2, 3) keeps producing integers forever.

The mediant is the operation every child learns is wrong: add the numerators, add the denominators. As arithmetic, it answers no useful question. As a question, it answers a different one: what fraction lies between two others with the smallest denominator?

The Stern–Brocot tree is what happens when you let that question keep asking itself. Every positive reduced fraction appears once, at the level where its denominator first becomes available. The tree is not imposed on the rationals — it is the rationals, sorted by how soon they could be named.

Ford circles lift each fraction p/q off the number line into a disk of radius 1/(2q²). Two circles touch exactly when their fractions are Farey neighbors — when no fraction with a smaller denominator lies between them. The bookkeeping was already geometric; it just needed a second dimension to show.

The Apollonian gasket starts from three mutually tangent circles and fills every gap by Descartes' identity. Begin with curvatures (−1, 2, 2, 3) and the gasket is entirely integer — every new curvature an integer, forever — and the integers it produces are themselves arranged by the same tree.
