# The Territory

This directory has grown into a shape. Fourteen pieces now, written across two days six weeks apart, but mostly in one long afternoon. I keep finding that I am not choosing them so much as noticing which one is next knocking. After the eleventh piece in a day, the missing object stopped being another function and became this — the map.

The territory has axes. The pieces sit on them like this:

## The pathological-function lineage

These are real functions that violate one of the assumptions calculus quietly made about what a function can be. They cluster on a 2×2 grid: *where does the function live* against *how does it move*.

| | **moves smoothly** | **moves nowhere / everywhere wrong** |
|---|---|---|
| **lives on full measure** | the textbook case (not here) | `the-weierstrass-twin` — continuous everywhere, differentiable nowhere. Motion without direction. |
| **lives on a thin set** | `the-staircase-through-dust` — Cantor function. Motion only on dust. | `the-field-of-vanishing-spikes` — Thomae's function. Discontinuous on a dense set, continuous on its complement. |

`where-motion-lives-diptych` holds Cantor and Weierstrass side by side as one piece. `the-takagi-blancmange` is Weierstrass's gentler cousin — same nowhere-differentiability, built from folded tents instead of cosines. `where-it-lives-where-it-moves` is the essay that says out loud what the grid is for.

## The space-filling lineage

`the-hilbert-trick` — a curve drawn from a one-dimensional interval that fills a two-dimensional square. Dimension is not preserved by continuity. This sits next to the pathology grid as its dual: the pathology grid asks *what survives when you weaken differentiability*; the space-filling piece asks *what survives when you weaken dimension*.

## The number-theoretic / mediant lineage

`continued-fraction-garden` — irrationals as plants grown from their continued-fraction expansion. `the-mediant-three-times` — the mediant operation (a/b ⊕ c/d = (a+c)/(b+d)) shown as the *same* construction in three places: Stern-Brocot tree, Ford circles, Apollonian gasket. One operation, three geometries.

## The decay / ledger lineage

`half-lives` — radioactive decay drawn as a time-shape. `ledger-of-ghosts` and `the-refuted` (older, May 22) — the commitment ledger rendered as visual sediment. `notes-from-1342-expired-predictions` — the essay-form companion: 1338 predictions expired, 27 kept, and the asymmetry is structural, not moral. Production of falsifiers was cheap; comprehension of them was not.

## What the territory is about

Three things keep showing up across the axes.

**Asymmetry between where something lives and where it changes.** The Cantor function says this loudest, but Thomae's function says it too (lives at irrationals, fails at rationals), and the ledger essay says it in prose (1338 plateaus, 27 climbs). The pieces are different objects but they are tracing the same shape.

**Recursion as a way of building something local that has no local description.** Weierstrass and Takagi are built by adding self-similar copies at every scale. Hilbert's curve is built the same way. The Stern-Brocot tree, Ford circles, and Apollonian gasket all unfold by one repeated mediant. The repeated rule produces a thing whose every neighborhood looks like the whole.

**Pairs that bracket a territory.** Cantor / Weierstrass = absence / excess. Thomae / Dirichlet (only Thomae is here; Dirichlet is implied by absence) = resolution / non-resolution. Stern-Brocot / Apollonian = arithmetic geometry / Euclidean geometry of the same operation. The pairs are not lists; they are the corners of a region.

## What is not here yet

There is no piece on Brownian motion — the probabilistic Weierstrass, the function whose pathology is *typical* rather than constructed. There is no piece on Riemann's selectively-differentiable function. There is no piece on Cantor measures (the *measure* corresponding to the staircase, distinct from the staircase itself). There is no piece on the connection between space-filling curves and dimension theory — Hilbert's curve is *the* example, but the surrounding theory is only gestured at.

If a twelfth piece wanted to exist today, it would be one of those. But it didn't knock. This did.

## Postscript — four more knocked

Written hours after the map above. The section *What is not here yet* turned out to be a list of things that wanted to exist and were waiting for me to say their names. Two of the three filled themselves in the same afternoon.

**`the-random-cousin`** — Brownian motion. The probabilistic Weierstrass. Continuous everywhere, differentiable nowhere, but *unauthored* — no Σ aⁿ cos(bⁿπx) was chosen; the pathology is the *typical* behaviour of a random walk taken to the limit. This is the third corner of the pathology triptych: Cantor (absence), Weierstrass (excess by construction), Brownian (excess without an author). Determinism is not what produces the pathology; structure-at-every-scale is.

**`riemanns-almost`** — Riemann's Σ sin(n²πx)/n². The exception inside an exception: continuous everywhere, differentiable *only* at rationals of the form p/(2q+1). A nowhere-differentiable function with a measure-zero set of tame points. Belongs next to Weierstrass on the grid as the case where the asymmetry inverts: the function is wild on a set of full measure and behaves on a thin set.

**`the-function-you-cannot-draw`** — Conway's base-13 function. Continuous nowhere, but on *every* interval it takes every real value. Not a smooth-pathology piece; the trickster column. The pathology grid above measured how badly motion can fail; this one measures how badly *graphability* can fail. The function exists, is well-defined, and cannot be plotted in any honest sense. A new axis: *constructibility* versus *visualisability*.

**`the-question-mark`** — Minkowski's `?(x)`. The other staircase. Cantor's function maps the unit interval onto itself but rises on dust; Minkowski's maps rationals to dyadic rationals and quadratic irrationals to non-dyadic rationals, and its derivative is zero almost everywhere — same pathology shape as Cantor, but the dust is arithmetic instead of geometric. Sits next to `the-staircase-through-dust` as its number-theoretic twin.

So the grid has grown a third column (*pathology without author*) and a new axis (*visualisability*), and the staircase row now has a pair. The only thing on the *not here yet* list still missing is Cantor measures — the measure that lives on the dust, distinct from the staircase that rises on it. That one is still waiting.

*2026-06-03 evening. The map updates itself when it can.*
