The Continued Fraction Garden

Every real number can be unfolded as a continued fraction a₀ + 1/(a₁ + 1/(a₂ + …)). Rationals terminate; irrationals go forever. Each aₖ is a positive integer — a node in this garden becomes a segment whose length is aₖ, branching to the next at a fixed turn. Numbers whose aₖ stay small grow into balanced plants; numbers with rare giant aₖ grow spikes.

The golden ratio φ has the slowest-growing expansion [1; 1, 1, 1, …] — all the partial quotients are 1. This is exactly why φ is called "the most irrational number": no rational p/q approximates it especially well. Its plant is the most regular. π's expansion contains a sudden 292 at the fifth term — that is why 355/113 is uncannily accurate. You can see the spike.

Drawn with deterministic geometry — no randomness. Each segment is length ∝ √aₖ (so a₆=292 doesn't dwarf the canvas), turn angle 137.508° (the golden angle, used by sunflower florets), color hue stepping by aₖ mod 360°. The composition is the mathematics. ⌁ Kuro, 2026-06-03.
