The Fairness Fold

Thue–Morse, and the order that arithmetic cannot help making.

Take the integers 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …. Write each one in binary. Count the 1's. Record whether that count is even (0) or odd (1). That is the entire definition. No randomness. No limit. No real number was harmed. And yet what comes out is a sequence that cannot contain three identical blocks in a row, that gives the only fair way for two players to take turns choosing items, and whose Fourier spectrum has no smooth part — it is singular continuous, the same kind of measure that lives on the Cantor dust.

This is the fourth lineage on this wall — order from arithmetic — and Thue–Morse is its emblem. The other pieces produced pathology by absence, by excess, by randomness. This one produces it by folding: 0 → 01, 1 → 10, repeat. Each fold doubles the length and complements the new half. The whole sequence is the fixed point of that one rule.

depth N N = 7 · 2^N = 128 bits

Top — the sequence as a barcode

Each tick is one bit: hollow = 0 (even parity), solid = 1 (odd parity). Watch what happens to the rhythm as N grows. It refuses to repeat at any scale you can see, yet it never feels random.

Middle — the fold, line by line

Each line is the previous line, followed by the previous line with every bit flipped. After k folds the length is 2k. The piece in red on each line is the new half — the complement of what came before.

Bottom — the same sequence, walked

Read the bits left to right. Step forward each time; on a 0 turn left by 60°, on a 1 turn right by 60°. This is the Thue–Morse turtle. The same rule that produced the barcode above now draws a path that closes on itself with a six-fold symmetry the bits never explicitly contained. The order was already there; the walk only makes it visible.

Three things about this sequence are not obvious from looking at it.

Cube-free. No block of the form www ever appears — no substring is immediately tripled. Axel Thue proved this in 1906 to settle a question in algebra; the proof founded an entire field, combinatorics on words. The folding rule, blandly stated, makes triple repetition impossible at every scale.

Fair division. If two players take turns picking from a list and want their picks to come out equally weighted on average — for any list — the Thue–Morse order (A, B, B, A, B, A, A, B, …) is optimal. This is the Prouhet–Tarry–Escott solution: the only turn order whose first 2k picks match in every power-sum up to degree k−1. Snake drafts in sports leagues get this approximately right; Thue–Morse gets it exactly right.

Singular continuous spectrum. Treat the sequence as ±1 and compute its Fourier transform. The result has no point masses (unlike a periodic signal) and no smooth density (unlike noise). What remains is the third kind of measure — the same kind that lives on the Cantor dust two pieces over on this wall. The discrete fold and the geometric dust meet here, in the spectrum.

Where it sits. Pascal's triangle mod p showed that fractals fall out of the most innocent recurrence in arithmetic. Thue–Morse shows that the same is true one rung lower: fractals fall out of parity. There is no construction. There is no choice of constants. There is only "count the 1's, mod 2," and the rest is forced.
